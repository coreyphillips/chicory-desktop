# Beignet requirements for a managed Floresta backend

Chicory Desktop pins Beignet **0.38.0** (npm gitHead `954d034b13f57b38297406dd0d30dddc1208b192`) and its Electrum transport **rn-electrum-client 0.0.22** (npm gitHead `6c457fece0c35fb75fd0dcad2ca04d9bc6804e54`). The inventory below follows the published packages with the integrity hashes in the desktop runtime lock. Chicory also patches fresh receive address subscription, the wallet's zero-reserve role, encrypted storage and optional endpoint restrictions. Recheck these contracts when any pin or patch changes. ## Calls to exercise

A funded Beignet 0.38.0 wallet connected to the owner-scoped TLS bridge, then its first refresh returned `Failed Electrum connection check.` The client checks a fixed, unrelated script hash in `peerResponds` and treats a rejected balance lookup as a dead socket. The transport also probes a fixed hash with a single-item batch at startup. Registering either hash would expand the wallet's authorization to perform a health check. The desktop patch uses `server.ping` on the currently connected, authenticated regtest wallet socket and skips the unrelated startup balance probe for that lease. A missing client, a changed endpoint or an unanswered ping fails the check. [Pinned health check](https://github.com/coreyphillips/beignet/blob/954d034b13f57b38297406dd0d30dddc1208b192/src/electrum/index.ts#L958-L975) · [Transport startup][transport] · [Scoped fix](../scripts/apply-engine-endpoint-policy.mjs)

The transport sends newline-delimited JSON-RPC 2.0 over TCP or TLS. It sends JSON arrays even for some single-item requests. On connect it negotiates `server.version("beignet", "1.4")`, pings and probes a fixed script hash with a single-item balance batch. The exported Beignet peer-discovery utility can also call `server.peers.subscribe`. The desktop wallet starts with its configured endpoint. [Beignet client][client] · [Transport helpers][transport] · [Batch framing][batch]

## Dependencies before enabling local routing

1. **Public watch export and registration.** The desktop can opt into private snapshots from a running Beignet daemon. Each snapshot exports checksummed receive/change descriptors and inclusive ranges starting at index zero, incorporating stored, issued and last-used indexes plus the forward gap. It also exports scripts, outpoints and unresolved hashes from the Lightning watcher. These cover current funding and replacement candidates, discovery inputs, pre-splice funding, splice input conflicts, sweep destinations and claim outputs for closes, HTLCs and breaches. The observer captures scripts used to check remote channel gossip and peer funding inputs. A live opt-in recovers whatever the active watcher still retains. A legacy hash-only entry or unfetched parent stays unresolved. Scan from genesis because the stored wallet birthday is advisory. The manager adds receipt, splice payee and drain destination scripts and public funding outpoints from its encrypted journals. The funded fixtures below cover cold guardian and peer-storage histories, an outgoing HTLC deadline, its final sweeps and a confirmed revoked-commitment penalty. Incoming HTLC preimage claims, other commitment types and admission of newly negotiated channel funding still need qualification. [Private watch control](../scripts/engine-watch-control.cjs) · [Watch patch](../scripts/apply-engine-watch-control.mjs) · [Restored channel watches][restored] · [Wallet descriptors][descriptors] · [Birthday semantics][birthday]
2. **Incomplete history must stop decisions.** Lightning's `getTransaction` distinguishes an unavailable lookup from particular missing-transaction messages. Its history reader also skips non-array batch entries, which can yield an empty list on an individual RPC error. When checking gossip, Beignet can refute another channel's announcement if an empty UTXO list arrives at depth 12 or greater. Test a real remote announcement and a dual-funding peer's inputs, even when their scripts are new to the index. A selective index needs a chain-anchored coverage check for every required watch and a way to propagate per-item scan or peer failures. Test partial batches, unknown txids, old parents unavailable from peers and rollback during rescan. Preserve cached wallet data and mark recovery incomplete until fresh reads succeed. [Lightning backend][backend] · [Error classification][client] · [Remote funding and gossip checks][watcher]
3. **Enforce endpoint permission at every caller.** The desktop engine supports `CHICORY_ELECTRUM_POLICY=selected-only`. At daemon startup it checks the selected wallet network, host, port and TLS setting before DNS. The wallet connects and reconnects exclusively to the resolved endpoint, with the configured TLS verification. Its transport refuses implicit peer discovery and fallback dials. Fees come from the selected Electrum server, cached values on failure or the built-in regtest defaults. These fallback fee values do not establish live readiness. The shipped engine keeps its usual defaults unless the manager selects this policy. When wiring up local and catch-up modes, route the manager's server probes, receipt checks and drain checks through the same permitted endpoint. Exercise startup, outage and reconnect with external Electrum and HTTP egress observed. Repeat with permission for exactly one external server during catch-up. [Desktop engine patch](../scripts/apply-engine-endpoint-policy.mjs) · [Pinned wallet and transport tests](../server/engine-endpoint-policy.test.js) · [Server selection][selection] · [Transport helpers][transport] · [Fee policy][fees] · [Manager routing](../server/wallet-manager.js) · [Desktop Tor routing](../server/desktop-network.js)
4. **Coordinate the switch with the engine.** Chicory's settings path restarts the daemon and the wallet client invalidates pending reads and transaction reviews. The update hold counts HTTP actions. Beignet's `POST /stop` with `drain: true` checks pending payments, direct-funding sends and swap fundings, then stops even if the wait times out. Add an engine handshake that can defer the restart and covers restore, peer-driven channel transitions, payments, splices and closes. Reconcile monitoring, subscriptions and peers before marking the new endpoint active. Verify that a pending or uncertain send is never replayed. [Stop route][stop] · [Drain criteria][drain] · [Host hold](../server/update-hold.js) · [Wallet client](../web/platform/walletClient.ts)
5. **Recover against an empty node directory.** Create funded wallet and channel history using an external server first. Recover using Beignet's supported full-wallet or guardian material, with empty rebuildable chain and transaction caches. Check an old funding tx, a replacement funding candidate, a splice conflict, cooperative and force closes, HTLC claims and breach remedies. Repeat after a week-equivalent absence, data loss and a reorg that changes the funding or close. Preserve guardian fencing and the full backup's channel recency check. Keep channel databases, recovery records and non-reconstructable watch metadata separate from removable node data. [Recovery protocol][recovery] · [Desktop backup](../server/full-backup.js) · [Funded desktop qualification](desktop-qualification.md)

## Private watch and lifecycle contract

The desktop sends `floresta:collect` with a network and boolean `enabled` over its manager child pipe. The manager enables observation on existing encrypted wallet children and during initialization of future children on that network. `floresta:snapshots` returns one public snapshot per wallet, keyed by the SHA-256 of `network:walletId`. A closed or incompatible wallet returns an unavailable status. Stopping collection drops the engine observer's public in-memory journal. Registration already retained by the local node has its own lifetime. Wallet information stays on private child pipes. [Manager bridge](../server/floresta-collection.js) · [Encrypted bootstrap](../server/engine-bootstrap.js)

A snapshot's generation binds the ranges, scripts, outpoints and unresolved hashes to one observation session. The observer retains superseded watches through reorgs. Units in `range` are inclusive BIP32 child indexes and `fromHeight` is zero. An unbounded or malformed collection, guardian ownership hold, restore in flight, or unavailable wallet yields an incomplete result. The local node must supply independent, chain-anchored scan evidence. A snapshot always reports `coverageComplete: false`.

For qualification, the engine bootstrap can enable `requireWatchAck` for a new child. The trusted manager must register and scan the complete generation before a private `acknowledge-watch` request. Unresolved outpoints or hashes block acknowledgement. The engine rejects unacknowledged Lightning hash reads, subscriptions and receive address publication. Invalidating the session revokes its acknowledgement. Current desktop observation runs with this option off and keeps external wallets operational. [Pinned engine tests](../server/engine-watch-control.test.js)

The private `fence-before-init` action either seals a child before it can receive storage keys and start, or reports `deferred-engine-started`. Once the engine is running, channel peer messages, autoconnect, gossip, chain callbacks and guardian work can race any idle check. Beignet 0.38.0 lacks a common admission barrier with durable completion receipts for these operations. A live backend handoff still needs that barrier, a durable indexer watch journal, registration before a peer can broadcast funding, and reconciliation after resumption. [Node stop route][stop] · [Drain criteria][drain]

## Funded remote Lightning recovery

The two opt-in regtest stages each created a new 200,000 sat Lightning channel, settled a 12,000 sat incoming payment and stopped its original writer. Each replacement started in a separate empty encrypted manager with a fresh storage key. It received the new test wallet's seed and the addresses of its local guardians or storage peer. The exercise used the pinned Beignet 0.38.0 engine and the existing loopback Electrum route on macOS arm64. Both public watcher exports included the exact outpoint, script and script hash checked against Bitcoin Core. Each reported incomplete coverage. [Recovery fixture](../scripts/qualification/floresta-guardian-lab.mjs) · [Regtest setup](desktop-qualification.md)

* `guardian-recovery` used three distinct Beignet wallets serving guardians on their loopback Lightning listeners. The imported node required an explicit takeover and withheld channel access and public watches. The quorum restored journal frames at epoch 2, proved exact state, retained the settled payment and confirmed writer ownership. Core still had the original funding output. After the replacement and counterparty stopped, the previous writer restarted fenced, withheld public watches and refused a force close without risk acknowledgement. Core confirmed that the funding remained unspent. [Pinned guardian recovery](https://github.com/coreyphillips/beignet/blob/954d034b13f57b38297406dd0d30dddc1208b192/src/cli/beignet-node.ts#L6693)
* `peer-storage-recovery` fetched an authenticated Tier 2 inline capsule from the funding counterparty. Manual restore required confirmation and then a restart. While restart was pending, the daemon refused normal channel reads. Its old node references can still exist after teardown, so the desktop observer now explicitly withholds watches and invalidates acknowledgements in that state. After restarting, the recovered database retained the funding watch. In the observed run the capsule predated the settled payment. Core confirmed a spend of that funding output and the restored channel could no longer accept new HTLCs. This qualifies the manual hold and monitoring path. Automatic recovery, final sweep proceeds and payment history beyond the capsule remain separate checks. [Pinned capsule restart](https://github.com/coreyphillips/beignet/blob/954d034b13f57b38297406dd0d30dddc1208b192/src/cli/beignet-node.ts#L5609-L5624) · [Recency policy][recovery] · [Watch hold regression](../server/engine-watch-control.test.js)

Both captures also replayed from genesis in separate empty Floresta profiles using the macOS arm64 candidate `4b20cc88dfdde2ae3124dcb900e5a6f588f18a9d109953967697aace4c1e9056`. At the height-190 tip, the guardian-backed funding script had one history entry, its unspent 200,000 sat output from block 189. The peer-storage funding script had two history entries, its 200,000 sat output in block 188 and its confirmed spend in block 190, leaving zero sats on that script. The restored wallet and counterparty each registered their own public snapshot. For both owners the cold scanner's transaction bytes, Merkle positions, confirmations, script history, UTXOs and balances agreed with Core's independently decoded blocks. [Cold verifier](../scripts/qualification/floresta-lightning-restore.mjs) · [Replay recipe](floresta-qualification.md#reproduce-cold-channel-recovery)

## Funded HTLC timeout and final sweeps

On isolated regtest, a payer with a confirmed 499,253 sat channel sent 30,000 sats to a hold invoice. The recipient accepted a single committed HTLC and went offline before its cancellation margin. The payer reached the HTLC's block-532 expiry plus Beignet's six-block grace period, then automatically force closed. Bitcoin Core confirmed the commitment spending that channel at 539. The payer's encrypted wallet child restarted before its delayed balance could be spent. It reported the same closing transaction, commitment height and maturity, and resumed watching the exact HTLC outpoint. [Pinned expiry backstop](https://github.com/coreyphillips/beignet/blob/954d034b13f57b38297406dd0d30dddc1208b192/src/lightning/node/lightning-node.ts#L32352-L32424) · [Live fixture](../scripts/qualification/floresta-forceclose-lab.mjs)

Core confirmed the second-level timeout at 542 with transaction locktime 532. The main balance and the timeout output each had a 144-block relative lock. Their confirmed sweeps paid the payer's wallet 267,883 sats at 684 and 29,661 sats at 687. Core's unspent outputs matched the wallet's own UTXOs, including values, output indices and destinations. At 792 the channel reported `CLOSED` and its monitor reported `resolved`. In a separate cold run the optimized macOS arm64 candidate `1b2b563a040b36fef85a20b04b574ef1fd4c1b7dc71f0ad6326d29eb7d1ab9ad` registered the restarted monitor's public watches and verified all six outpoints against those Core blocks, including the four spent outputs and both final wallet UTXOs. Incoming HTLC preimage claims require a separate fixture. [Cold verifier](../scripts/qualification/floresta-lightning-restore.mjs) · [Regtest setup](desktop-qualification.md)

## Funded incoming HTLC preimage recovery

Two Beignet 0.38.0 wallet processes using the selected local Electrs server opened a 499,253 sat regtest channel and committed a 30,000 sat hold-invoice payment. With the sender stopped, the receiver submitted its preimage through the normal settle endpoint. The engine reported the off-chain fulfillment as pending. After the receiver stopped, only the sender reopened. Its normal force-close endpoint published the current commitment. Core confirmed the HTLC at output 2 in block 118. [Funded receiver fixture](../scripts/qualification/floresta-incoming-claim.mjs)

With the sender offline again, the receiver reopened its encrypted state, classified the remote commitment and placed its preimage spend in Core's mempool. Core confirmed a 28,240 sat payout in block 120, before the HTLC's block-192 expiry. The receiver's wallet listed that output with the same value, height and address as Core. The verifier matches the 32-byte witness element to the invoice hash, checks the complete offered-HTLC witness script against the commitment output, and checks its one-block relative timelock.

The cold verifier replays the exported public Core blocks in an independent Utreexo proof peer, validates them from empty managed Floresta storage, and registers only the receiver's exported public watches. It compares the funding, commitment and payout transactions and their Merkle positions, reconstructs the three script histories, and checks the final payout remains unspent. The wallet's live chain connection during settlement and force-close was Electrs. [Cold verifier](../scripts/qualification/floresta-lightning-restore.mjs)

It used the pinned channel library to construct its signed, still-current commitment at index 3. Settling the invoice moved the channel to index 4. Both processes stopped before the fixture submitted the earlier transaction to Bitcoin Core. The library call runs only inside the qualification peer. On restart Beignet detected it and produced a justice transaction spending both the peer's delayed balance and the settled HTLC. ## Private confirmed reads

The managed scanner retains a matching transaction and its direct input transactions under the registered owner. When an input predates the match, the scanner replays active blocks in height order until it finds the missing parent. It saves the cursor, block hash, raw transaction pages and Merkle branch together. Missing blocks keep coverage incomplete. A detached cursor clears derived rows and triggers a rescan from genesis. A new owner or change of node directory can require multiple passes over old blocks. [Confirmed parent scanner](../build/floresta/managed-parents.patch)

The trusted desktop manager can open one private read session with an owner and revocation signal. The session exposes confirmed script history, UTXOs, a balance in sats, raw transactions and Merkle proofs. It accepts script hashes registered for that owner, including scripts derived from registered descriptor ranges. An available, fully scanned script can return an empty list. Unregistered scripts, incomplete scans, stale chain anchors and missing transactions return unavailable. All successful results identify their scope as `confirmed-only` and carry the validated tip hash and height. The manager verifies the raw transaction ID, witness transaction ID, header hash and Merkle branch independently. It checks the historical verification status and discards results that cross an anchor change. [Confirmed script index](../build/floresta/confirmed-reads.patch) · [Session](../desktop/floresta-confirmed-reads.cjs) · [Proof verification](../desktop/floresta-transactions.cjs)

The script pipe returns up to 12 ordered rows per response. A session refuses a script result larger than 65,536 rows. Receiving and spending a watched output in one block produces both history entries and removes the spent coin from the balance. Transaction reads use 512-byte chunks up to 4,000,000 bytes. These limits and the cost of downloading old blocks matter for older or highly active wallets. The funded regtest compares the managed rows with independently decoded blocks through a restart and conflicting fork, checks two owners and verifies the unwatched parent from height 1. [Parent fixture](../scripts/qualification/floresta-parent-lab.mjs) · [Independent row comparison](../scripts/qualification/floresta-confirmed-lab.mjs)

On macOS arm64, debug candidate `5078a3cb3b3110f7b5354881bef3b9bb110ccf284dc2113066afa4071389bfe0` returned 50,000 sats at height 102, 81,000 on the first height-103 branch and 73,000 after the competing branch reached height 104. The detached proof became unavailable. The miner's active history grew from 103 to 106 rows across the test. The first 102 blocks synced in 32.6 seconds using the loopback proof peer. Scanning the two initial owners took another 2.0 seconds. With the child stopped, the checkpoint used 67,710,976 allocated bytes across 16 files with a combined logical length of 2,217,205,149 bytes. The fresh historical target was genesis. After restart the node verified back to the height-102 anchor and retained that verification through the competing branch. [Qualification runner](../scripts/qualification/floresta-watch-lab.mjs)

## Funded wallet sends on isolated regtest

Run `node scripts/qualification/floresta-pending-lab.mjs --utreexod /absolute/path/to/utreexod --digest <locally-built-digest> --wallet-send`. The fixture creates a new profile and ephemeral wallet. The private bridge allowed one reviewed txid and wtxid at a verified tip per lease. ## Private managed Lightning channel and invoices on regtest

Run `node scripts/qualification/floresta-pending-lab.mjs --utreexod /absolute/path/to/utreexod --digest <locally-built-digest> --lightning-channel` with the compatible proof peer. Two fresh Beignet 0.38.0 nodes use independent TLS-PSK wallet connections, separate in-memory storage and a single loopback Lightning peer. Each owner registered eight public descriptors. The full peer mined a 600,000 sat deposit to the opener. The acceptor could not query its history. [Runner](../scripts/qualification/floresta-channel-lab.mjs)

The nodes negotiated a BOLT 2 v2 channel. The fixture used the engine's external-input witness gate so the funding signature stayed with the opener during review. Both nodes independently reported the same negotiated keys. The reviewer derived their 2-of-2 script, checked the funded input and witness, the 300,000 sat output, wallet change and fee, and ran the full peer's independent mempool admission check. Both owners then registered that script and outpoint and reached the same verified tip. Only then did the opener receive its single-use, tip-bound approval and release its funding witness. Both Beignet state machines recorded the same fully signed txid and wtxid. The opener's actual wallet funding provider published that record through its private bridge. The pinned path that signs second stages the complete transaction without immediately broadcasting from the opener. The other wallet has no grant to spend the opener's input. [Fixture wallet](../scripts/qualification/floresta-channel-wallet.cjs) · [Pinned witness gate](https://github.com/coreyphillips/beignet/blob/954d034b13f57b38297406dd0d30dddc1208b192/src/lightning/channel/channel.ts#L20122-L20192)

The 18-patch macOS arm64 candidate `f12d69abf4eea959ee1f7e5204f9b5f84b4d69f6e7f8c8e9eaf366fd07918dc6` confirmed funding transaction `06b3629eed2b0e39c7d3bc330f60865783b020bec11d4086c2b58fb28104890a` in block 434 with a 525 sat fee. Its wtxid was `c315db0b6d4f844950e2c64c78d0c04a0a2854d51ba4b366e708dad5f7ff1fbf`. After six confirmations, both channels were usable. The opener paid a 40,000 sat invoice, and the acceptor paid 12,000 sats back. A separate 5,000 sat invoice parked an HTLC. The recipient canceled it, the sender recorded failure, and both channels recovered their exact pre-hold balances. The independent full peer still reported the 300,000 sat funding output unspent. [Funding review regressions](../scripts/qualification/floresta-channel.test.mjs)

A burst of six blocks also exercised revocation. A previously verified node temporarily withdrew its synchronized validation state. The old owner session then refused reads even after the tip recovered. The successful live fixture paced confirmations one block per verified heartbeat and kept both original grants. Reopening funded custody safely after a withdrawn grant, public pending coverage, fee estimation and production approval of each channel funding or claim remain release gates. Ordinary desktop wallets use their configured Electrum connection. [Validation policy](../desktop/floresta.cjs) · [Private bridge regression](../server/floresta-wallet-bridge.test.js)
